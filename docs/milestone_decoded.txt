Project Context

Your team is developing the preliminary aerodynamic-design and mission-analysis tools for a twin-rotor
tiltrotor aircraft. At this  stage, the rotors must be evaluated in  helicopter  mode and propeller-like  axial
forward flight. The same aerodynamic model must serve as the backend of a mission planner that tracks
aircraft mass, rotor limits, power demand, power available, and fuel use.

Learning Objectives

ò

Implement a flexible Blade Element Momentum Theory (BEMT) solver and establish numerical
credibility through validation.

ò  Understand the influence of blade geometry, collective pitch, rotational speed, and advance ratio on

rotor thrust, torque, power, efficiency, and stall.

ò  Select and justify a preliminary tiltrotor rotor design that performs acceptably in hover and axial

forward flight.

ò  Build the first version of a segment-based mission planner with continuous feasibility checks.

Tasks

1. BEMT tool

Develop  a  modular  BEMT  solver  for  a  rotor  with  selectable  blade  number,  radius,  root  cut-out,  radial
chord/taper,  radial  twist,  airfoil  data,  collective  pitch,  rotational  speed,  altitude,  temperature  offset,
climb/descent velocity, and axial forward velocity. Include iterative induced-velocity solution and Prandtl
tip loss; root loss may be included if justified.

2. Aerodynamic limits

Use angle-dependent airfoil lift and drag data or a documented model. Detect sectional stall and report
the radial/azimuthal location and fraction of the blade operating beyond the adopted stall criterion.

4. Design-variable study

For the validation rotor or another documented baseline, independently vary solidity, blade number, taper
ratio,  linear  twist,  root  cut-out,  and  rotational  speed.  Use  sufficient  points  to  reveal  trends  and  explain
changes.

5. Tiltrotor rotor design

Define a preliminary twin-rotor tiltrotor aircraft and select the rotor airfoil(s), radius, number of blades,
RPM schedule, chord distribution, twist distribution,  root cut-out,  and allowable collective range. State
disk loading, tip Mach number, solidity, and design constraints.

6. Hover assessment

Generate  thrust,  torque,  power,  stall  margin,  versus  collective  for  relevant  altitudes  and  gross  weights.
Establish hover ceiling or maximum hover weight subject to power and stall constraints.

7. Axial forward-flight / propeller assessment

Evaluate the rotor at several propeller advance ratios J and collective settings, with the rotor axis aligned
with  the  freestream.  Report  thrust  coefficient,  power  coefficient,  propulsive  efficiency,  blade-angle-of-
attack distribution, and feasible operating envelope. Identify a suitable cruise operating point.

8. Comparable-rotor benchmarking

Compare  the  proposed  rotor  against  at  least  two  comparable  tiltrotor,  proprotor,  or  large  propeller
datasets/designs  using  nondimensional  metrics.  Differences  in  size  and  operating  condition  must  be
normalized and discussed.

9. Mission Planner v1

Create a mission planner supporting hover, vertical climb/descent, axial airplane-mode cruise, loiter, and
payload  pickup/drop.  Update  fuel  and  gross  weight  at  user-defined  time  intervals.  Account  for  reserve
fuel,  atmospheric  variation,  wind  during  cruise,  engine/drive  losses,  and  an  engine  or  motor  power-
available model.

10. Feasibility warnings

At each time step, check fuel  availability, power  margin,  rotor stall,  tip Mach number, RPM/collective
bounds,  and  any  additional  adopted  design  limit.  Clearly  identify  the  segment,  time,  and  reason  for
mission failure.
